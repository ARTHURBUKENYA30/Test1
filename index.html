About the Museum

A living archive of Uganda's print history

The Uganda Printing & Publishing Museum exists to preserve, interpret and share the remarkable story of printing and publishing in Uganda.

From historic printing presses and typesetting equipment to newspapers, books, government publications, photographs and personal stories, the museum connects generations through the printed word.

  • Preserving historic printing machinery and equipment.
  • Documenting Uganda's publishing and journalism heritage.
  • Collecting rare newspapers, books and publications.
  • Educating students and future generations.
  • Celebrating the people behind Uganda's print industry.

Our Timeline

120+ years of printing in Uganda

A journey from early government printing to newspapers, publishing, commercial printing and the digital age.

1902

The Government Printer

The beginnings of Uganda's modern government printing tradition establish an important foundation for the country's print and publishing history.

Early 1900s

Print & Public Communication

Printing becomes increasingly important for official notices, education, public information and communication.

Mid 20th Century

Newspapers & Publishing

Newspapers, books and other publications become important channels for public debate, education and national development.

Independence Era

A Changing Nation

Uganda's publishing and printing industries continue evolving alongside the country's social, political and cultural changes.

Late 20th Century

Modern Printing Technology

New machinery and production techniques transform the way books, newspapers, government documents and commercial materials are produced.

21st Century

The Digital Revolution

Digital publishing, desktop design, online journalism and electronic communication reshape the world of print.
